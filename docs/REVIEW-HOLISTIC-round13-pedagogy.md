# Round 13: content and pedagogy review (HOLISTIC v12 + §19–§20, SP v6 + §21–§22)

## (1) Verdict: NOT CLEAN (3 majors, no blockers)

## (2) Substantive issues

**M1. §1.10 area table vs §8.4 / §1.6: bass and drums have no "line" or "harmony" area, so transfer evidence has nowhere to go. Severity: major.**
- §1.10 maps learn-a-tune transfer forms to "the instrument's line or harmony area". It also maps "unseen rule-generated licks at sight" to "the instrument's line-jazz area".
- The bass areas are scales, arpeggios/chord tones, walking/groove, reading and rhythm/time. The drum areas are rudiments, coordination, grooves/styles and reading. Neither instrument has a line-jazz or harmony area.
- Why it matters: learn-a-tune steps 6–8 and the §20 bass step 6 now feed level only through transfer forms. A builder has no valid area for the bass "new key at sight" walking take, the bass lick at sight, or the drum head at sight. Two outcomes are likely: the evidence gets dropped, which leaves bass and drums with no real-music route to rank, or it gets routed somewhere arbitrary. The guard tests can't check a mapping that doesn't exist.
- Fix: add explicit rows to the table.
  - Bass: melody or lick at sight → scales. Roots, guide tones and walking at sight → walking/groove. Chart at sight → walking/groove.
  - Drums: kicks/set-ups and trading inside the form → grooves/styles. Head or chart at sight → reading.
  - Add a guard test that every family × instrument resolves to an existing area or to coverage.

**M2. §8.7 accompanying a singer: the §20 reorder breaks the level references, and the source for level 6 is undefined. Severity: major.**
- §20 moves "transposing at sight (±2)" ahead of "following human rubato". But §8.7 still says "only level 4 needs a human take", and §8.9 lists "accompaniment L4" as "teacher or recording needed". After the reorder, L4 is transposing.
- Why it matters: a builder following the numbers will do two wrong things:
  - Label the measurable transposing level "teacher or recording needed". That makes it skippable and keeps it out of the level test.
  - Grade the rubato level against app playback. That is exactly the algorithmic-rubato path the no-claude-composed-music rule forbids.
- Level 6 ("endings with the singer's fermata") has no stated source. With app playback, the fermata length is fixed and can be memorised, so the learner isn't following anyone. If an app-chosen hold length were used to make it feel human, it would drift toward the rejected humanize layer.
- Fix:
  - Refer to levels by name, not number, in §8.7, §8.9 and §13.1.
  - Mark the fermata ending like rubato: human takes only, labelled "teacher or recording needed" until the bank exists. An alternative is to grade it only as release-on-cue against a notated, fixed hold, and say so explicitly.

**M3. §20 "Tunes you know" is self-contradictory. Severity: major (it is a grading rule).**
- §19 says the item is graded by intervals, any key, after a tonic reference. §20 says the starting degree is graded relative to the given tonic, and "the key of the sung phrase is free".
- If the starting degree must match the given tonic and the intervals are graded, the key is fixed: it is the reference key. It is not free.
- Why it matters: the builder has to choose between rejecting correct transposed singing and ignoring the starting degree.
- Fix: pick one rule.
  - (a) The tonic reference is played, then the phrase is graded on starting degree plus intervals in that key. The octave is free.
  - (b) No reference is played. The phrase is graded on intervals only and the learner's own implied tonic is inferred. The starting degree is then graded against that inferred tonic.
