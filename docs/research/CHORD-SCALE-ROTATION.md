# Chord-scale rotation in rule-based improvisation: research (2026-11-22)

**Question.** The owner wants a mode for rule-based improvisation: each chord gets a scale, the app swaps that scale from time to time (V7: altered, Lydian dominant, HM5, half-whole...; I: Ionian, Lydian, major bebop, 6th-diminished...), and logical substitutions on half bars bring in more scales. He says this is "how Barry Harris taught and how they practise at Open Studio and elsewhere".

**Short answer.**
- **The exercise is sound and mainstream.** It belongs to the chord-scale tradition (Aebersold, Levine, Berklee). That tradition orders the scales for each chord from mild to tense and tells students to work down the list.
- **It is not Barry Harris's method.** On record, Harris called modes "nonsense" and said they have "nothing to do with jazz" (§4.1). His material is the major, dominant and minor scales with half-step rules, chord conversion and the 6th-diminished scales.
- **There is a Harris version of the rotation idea.** It rotates the *chord you think* over a V7 (the related dominants, the important minor, the tritone's minor), the *starting degree* and the *rule set*. It does not rotate modes. §4.4 specifies it. The app should offer it as a separate "Harris" track and never label chord-scale rotation as Harris.
- **Open Studio.** Its public Harris courses (Chris Parks) are explicitly non-modal. The paid course content of Peter Martin and Adam Maness could not be checked (§3.5).

All examples are in C (or around G7 → C / G7 → Cm) unless a row says otherwise.

## 1. Chord-scale menus per chord function

**How to read the tables.**
- **Inside / Outside.** Inside = the scale adds only diatonic or "available" tensions, so it sounds consonant against the basic chord. Outside / tension = it adds altered tensions that must be heard as tension (and resolved).
- **Order.** The ranking follows Aebersold's "degree of dissonance" ordering, from mild at the top to tense lower down [V AEB].
- **Avoid note.** A scale note a ♭9 above a chord tone. Sustained or on a strong beat, it clashes with the chord, so it is used only as a passing note or one that resolves down by step [C; LEV-R ¶7, ¶9].

### 1.1 Major 7 / 6 (I and IV)

| Scale (in C) | Notes | Inside? | Avoid / conflict | Idiomatic context | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ionian** | C D E F G A B | inside | **F** (11, a ♭9 above E) | I resolving from V; "don't emphasize the 4th" | [V AEB; LEV-R ¶7] |
| **Major pentatonic** | C D E G A | inside | none (drops F and B) | Safe default, melodic; beginner tier | [V AEB] |
| **Lydian** | C D E F♯ G A B | inside-coloured | none | IV chord (Fmaj7 in C = F Lydian is diatonic). On I it gives a modern/ECM colour, and the comp can use maj7♯11. | [V AEB "C∆+4"; C] |
| **Major bebop** | C D E F G G♯ A B | inside (+1 passing) | F as above; G♯ is a passing note between G and A | Eighth-note lines with chord tones on beats | [V AEB] |
| **6th-diminished (Harris)** | C D E F G G♯ A B | inside | same pitch set as major bebop. The concept differs: C6 notes and B°7 notes alternate. | Harris-school lines and harmony; see §4 | [V AEB pitch set; BHM] |
| **Harmonic major** | C D E F G A♭ B | outside-ish | A♭ against A in C6 | Colour on I or IV (iv-minor flavour) | [V AEB] |
| **Lydian augmented** | C D E F♯ G♯ A B | outside | G♯ vs G | maj7♯5 chords only | [V AEB] |
| **Blues** (over major) | C E♭ F F♯ G B♭ | outside (blue) | E♭/B♭ against E/B | Blues/soul contexts only | [V AEB] |

- **Note.** Aebersold even lists the diminished scale (half-whole) over a major chord, and the Rawlins review calls that an unlikely choice [V LEV-R fn. 4].
- **App recommendation.** Leave it out of the I rotation.

### 1.2 Minor 7 (ii, iii, vi; i in a Dorian context)

| Function | Default scale | Notes (example) | Avoid | Alternatives | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ii** (Dm7 in C) | Dorian | D E F G A B C | Sources differ on **B** (13). Berklee-style teaching flags it on a *ii* because B–F is the V7 tritone. Levine treats Dorian as having no avoid note. | Dorian bebop (adds C♯ passing: D E F G A B C C♯); minor pentatonic; Harris: play G7's scale (§4) | [C] Berklee/Levine split; bebop and pentatonic [V AEB] |
| **iii** (Em7 in C) | Phrygian | E F G A B C D | **F** (♭9) and **C** (♭13) | E Dorian (F♯ is outside the key, fine when Em7 acts as a ii of D); often treated as a substitute for I (Cmaj9 without root) | [C] |
| **vi** (Am7 in C) | Aeolian | A B C D E F G | **F** (♭13) | A Dorian (F♯ brightens it; common when Am7 is a ii of G) | [C] |
| **i in a modal tune** (So What, Impressions) | Dorian | D E F G A B C | none (the 6th is the colour) | Minor pentatonic, Dorian bebop | [V AEB; C] |

- **Aebersold's minor order.** Aebersold: "the order of preference" for minor is "Dorian, Bebop, Melodic, Blues, Pentatonic", and pure (Aeolian) minor "is not used very often" [V AEB note 4].

### 1.5 Dominant 7

The order, from inside to outside, follows Aebersold's dominant list [V AEB]. The "context" column is the common-practice usage [C, unless tagged].

| Scale (on G) | Notes | Tensions | Avoid | Typical context | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mixolydian** | G A B C D E F | 9, 13 | **C** (11, a ♭9 above B) | V7 → I in major; IV7 and I7 in blues | [V AEB; LEV-R ¶9] |
| **Bebop dominant** | G A B C D E F F♯ | 9, 13 + passing | C as above | Eighth-note lines; chord tones on beats | [V AEB] |
| **Major pentatonic** | G A B D E | 9, 13 | none | Simple melodic choice | [V AEB] |
| **Lydian dominant** (4th mode of D melodic minor) | G A B C♯ D E F | 9, ♯11, 13 | none | **Non-resolving or non-fifth-moving dominants:** tritone subs (D♭7 → C), backdoor ♭VII7 (B♭7 → C), ♭VI7, II7 that holds (*Take the A Train* bar 3: D7♯11), IV7 in blues as a colour, 7♯11 voicings | [V AEB "C7+4"; C for context] |
| **Mixolydian ♭13** ("Hindu", 5th mode of melodic minor) | G A B C D E♭ F | 9, ♭13 | C | V7 → minor with a natural 9 | [V AEB "C7♭6"] |
| **Phrygian dominant / HM5** (5th mode of C harmonic minor) | G A♭ B C D E♭ F | ♭9, ♭13 | **C** (11) | **V7 → i minor**; secondary dominants to minor chords (V/ii, V/iii, V/vi) | [V AEB "Spanish or Jewish scale" C7♭9; C] |
| **Half-whole diminished** | G A♭ B♭ B C♯ D E F | ♭9, ♯9, ♯11, 13 | none (symmetric; the natural 5 and 13 are its colour) | V7 with **13♭9** voicings, or 7♭9 with natural 5. Common to major; to minor, the 13 (E♮) contradicts the key's E♭. | [V AEB "C7♭9 (also has ♯9 & ♯4)"; C] |
| **Whole tone** | G A B C♯ D♯ F | 9, ♯11, ♯5 | none | 7♯5 / 9♯5 / 7♭5 voicings; Monk-style passages | [V AEB "C7+"] |
| **Altered** (diminished whole tone / Super-Locrian; 7th mode of A♭ melodic minor) | G A♭ B♭ B D♭ E♭ F | ♭9, ♯9, ♯11 (♭5), ♭13 (♯5) | none | V7 → i minor (most idiomatic); also V7 → I in bebop and hard bop as maximum tension; 7♯9 / 7alt voicings | [V AEB "C7+9"; V LEV-R ¶17] |
| **Blues** | G B♭ C C♯ D F | ♯9 (blue 3rd) | — | Blues tunes | [V AEB] |

**Equivalences the app should know (pitch-set identities).**

| Pair | Why it matters | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| G altered = D♭ Lydian dominant = A♭ melodic minor | The tritone sub's natural scale *is* the original V's altered scale. Rotating "G7 altered" and "D♭7 Lydian dominant" tests the same notes over different bass notes. | [C; derivation checked] |
| G half-whole = B♭, D♭, E half-whole (one octatonic set) | The four "related dominants" share one diminished scale. That is Harris's "family" (§4). | [C; BHM] |
| G HM5 = C harmonic minor | "Phrygian dominant" is just the minor key's scale from its 5th | [C] |
| C major bebop = C6-dim (Harris); C bebop-minor-2 = Cm6-dim (Harris) | Same pitches, different concept (Harris alternates 6-chord and dim chord tones on the beats) | [V AEB; BHM] |

### 1.6 Secondary dominants

The Berklee-style rule: chord tones + the key's diatonic notes for the other degrees, adjusted where a ♭9 clash forbids it [C, Nettles & Graf; not page-checked]. In C major:

| Chord | Target | Diatonic-rule scale | Common jazz alternatives | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A7 = V7/ii** | Dm7 | A Mixolydian ♭13 (A B C♯ D E F G) | A HM5 (B♭: A B♭ C♯ D E F G), A altered, A half-whole | [C] |
| **B7 = V7/iii** | Em7 | B HM5 (B C D♯ E F♯ G A) | B altered | [C] |
| **C7 = V7/IV** | Fmaj7 | C Mixolydian (C D E F G A B♭) | C half-whole, C altered | [C] |
| **D7 = V7/V** | G7 | D Mixolydian (D E F♯ G A B C) | D Lydian dominant (G♯ is outside the key; very common), D altered | [C] |
| **E7 = V7/vi** | Am7 | E HM5 (E F G♯ A B C D) | E altered | [C] |

- **The design rule that follows.** The default for a dominant depends on whether its *target* is major (Mixolydian family) or minor (♭13 family: HM5, altered). The rotation menu should be filtered by target quality, not by the chord symbol alone.

## 2. Substitutions that are logical on a half bar

### 2.1 What makes a substitution "logical"

A sub on the second half of a bar is musical when it meets all of these. It is arbitrary when it fails them.

| Criterion | Logical | Arbitrary | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Target** | It approaches the *next* chord by a fifth down, a half step (down or up), or the backdoor whole step up | It doesn't lead to what follows (e.g., D♭7 followed by Dm7 instead of C) | [C] |
| **Guide tones** | It keeps the original chord's 3rd/7th (tritone sub) or moves them by half step into the target's 3rd/7th | The guide tones jump with no voice-leading reason | [C; BL treats lines as outlines between chords] |
| **Bass** | Chromatic or circle-of-fifths motion | Random leaps | [C] |
| **Melody** | The melody note at that moment is a chord tone or available tension of the sub (e.g., a held E♭ over the D♭7 sub = its 9, fine) | The melody note clashes with the sub (e.g., a held C over D♭7 = its major 7th) | [C] |
| **Density** | One sub per 2–4 bars at lower tiers | Back-to-back subs on every half bar before the player can hear the original | [?] (pedagogical choice) |

## 3. Pedagogy: how this is actually drilled

### 3.4 Barry Harris's practice model (summary; detail in §4)

- Practise the major, dominant and minor scales with the half-step rules from different starting points.
- Practise "scale outlines" of tunes (one scale per bar, up from the root).
- Compose and practise lines over specific chord moves, then apply them to tunes.

All of this is [V BHM; R1 "Scale practice or 'the ABC's'", "The Harris Half-Step Practice Model"; R2 "Rhythm Changes Scale Outline"...]. None of it is phrased as modes.

## 4. Barry Harris: what he actually taught about chord-scales

### 4.1 His own words on modes (primary source)

From the Smithsonian NEA Jazz Masters oral history, 20 August 2010, transcript pp. 25–26 [V SI]:

> "I never knew a mode in my life and I still learned to play jazz... I learned to play jazz without thinking about modes. I don't want to know a mode. They're talking about the Phrygian, the dorian, the mixolydian, and all that kind of nonsense... it has nothing to do with jazz, particularly."

In the same passage:
- He attributes mode teaching to schools needing "something" to teach.
- He rejects the "half-step, whole-step" description of the diminished scale. He derives it instead from a diminished chord plus the four dominants obtained by lowering each of its notes (crediting Schoenberg) [V SI].
- A YouTube short with a similar line ("never heard anything about a Dorian, or a Mixolydian, or a Lydian") is cited by M. Amendola, <https://michaelamendola.substack.com/p/i-never-heard-anything-about-a-dorian>. I did not watch the video [?].

### 4.3 Verdict on the owner's claim

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Rotate modes over each chord: this is how Barry Harris taught" | **Not his method.** Harris rejected mode thinking in his own words [V SI]. ### 5.5 How to grade "stayed in the assigned scale"

**Note classes (per detected note, against the scale active at its onset):**

| Class | Definition |
|---|---|
| **CT** | Chord tone of the written chord (Track B: of the thought chord) |
| **T** | Available tension = a scale note that is not an avoid note |
| **K** | Colour note: a scale note absent from the default scale for that function (Lydian: ♯11; HM5: ♭9, ♭13; altered: ♭9, ♯9, ♭13; Locrian ♮2: 9; half-whole: ♭9, ♯9, 13). K is a subset of T. |
| **A** | Avoid note of that scale (§1 tables) |
| **P** | Out of the set, *and* off the beat, *and* short (≤ an eighth), *and* moves by half step to an in-set note (the bebop/Harris passing allowance) |
| **W** | Any other out-of-set note |

**Timing tolerance.**
- A note within an eighth before a chord change may belong to the next chord, since anticipation is idiomatic.
- A note within ~60 ms after the change may belong to the previous chord.
- Choose the more favourable classification.

**Metrics.**

| Metric | Formula | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **In-set rate** | (CT + T + A + P) / all notes, weighted by duration | Basic "stayed in the scale" |
| **Strong-beat quality** | Share of beat-1/3 notes that are CT (or T at T3+) | Chord tones on downbeats |
| **Avoid handling** | An A that sits on a strong beat *and* lasts ≥ 1 beat, *or* doesn't move by step, counts as a fault | Levine/Berklee "handle with care"; the reviewer's resolution point [LEV-R] |
| **Colour coverage** | Share of chord spans (≥ 2 beats) where the player sounded ≥ 1 K note | **Essential.** Without it, a player who plays only chord tones "passes" every scale, so the rotation tests nothing. (Mixolydian and Lydian dominant differ only in the 4th.) |
| **Target resolution** (T3+) | Share of chord changes where the first note of the new chord is a CT reached by step or half step | Voice leading across the change [C; BL] |
| **Constraint compliance** | Per active rule (e.g., "bar starts on the 3rd") | The rule-based layer |

**Track B grader differences.**
- P is generous: any half step off the beat that resolves.
- CT is computed against the thought chord, not the written one.
- There is no colour metric. Instead, the thought chord's identity notes must appear (e.g., A♭ + E♭ for the tritone's minor over G7).

**Input.**
- MIDI keyboard: grade above a split point (right hand); left-hand comping is ignored.
- Monophonic instruments and voice: pitch-tracked notes, with an onset quantised to the 1/16 grid for beat position.
- (A design consequence for the app: the existing hands-free/MIDI pipeline is enough; no new detection is needed.) [?]
