### A1. The solo-piano framework: three elements, three registers, the "mango problem"

Source: PSJP 2nd ed. Chapter A (free PDF) and the PianoWithJonny summary.

**Three elements and their registers:**
- **Bass:** as low as possible, roughly the bottom two octaves. The lowest A, B♭ and B are rarely used. Rhythmically
  the bass defines the strong beats: half notes for a two-feel, quarters for a walk.
- **Chords:** the essential definition sits between **C3 and G4**. Below that it is muddy; above it is pretty but does
  not define the harmony. Chords can expand upward for colour. Comping is mostly off the beat in bebop and later styles;
  he compares it to a boxer's jabs.
- **Melody:** roughly the two octaves above middle C. Think of a singer or a horn. The melody is always
  **personalized**. Rhythmic freedom in the melody opens gaps where the RH can drop into the middle register and cover
  chords.
- **Top two octaves:** special effects only (bell tones, doubled melody, crystalline arpeggios).

**The mango problem.** Three mangos (bass, chords, melody), two hands. His five solutions double as his chapter map:

| # | Solution | Styles | PSJP chapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two in the LH, one in the RH | Stride, bass shells, every LH style that shuttles between bass and chord | Ch. 1–6 |
| 2 | Two in the RH, one in the LH | Continuous bassline in the LH; RH shuttles between melody and chords, harmonizes under the melody, or implies chords melodically | Ch. 7 |
| 3 | One in each hand, the third shared | Shared-hands voicings (5–8 notes), mainly for ballads | Ch. 9–16 |
| 4 | Drop a mango | Bass only, melody only, or most often **no bass** for long stretches | Ch. 8 |
| 5 | Juggle | Switch which hand covers what. Dave McKenna's "third hand approach". The great pianists mix freely. | throughout |

### A2. Three positions of the left hand (PSJP Ch. B, free PDF)

| Position | Register | Forms | C example (mine) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bass notes** | as low as possible | Single note; root + 5th (strong and open, his preferred harmonization); octaves only as an effect | C1/C2; C2+G2 |
| **Bass shells** | about C2–G4, essential tones (3rd, 7th) kept between C3 and G4 | See the shell forms below the table | — |
| **Rootless voicings** | around middle C | See the rootless notes below the table | — |

**Bass-shell forms:**
- **Shells:** just the 3rd and 7th. Type A has the 3rd below; Type B has the 7th below. Choose for the smoothest voice
  leading.
  - C example: Cmaj7 A = E3+B3; Cmaj7 B = B2+E3.
- **Two-note bass shells, "Bud Powell voicings":** the root plus *one* essential tone. The 3rd can sit a 3rd or a
  **10th** above the root; the 10th gets a lower, richer bass.
  - C examples: C3+E3, C2+E3 (10th), C3+B3 (7th).
  - ii–V–I starting with the 3rd: D3+F3 → G2+F3 → C3+E3.
  - ii–V–I starting with the 7th: D3+C4 → G2+B2 → C3+B3 (my voicing, **verify** against Ex. B.3).
- **Can't reach a 10th:** add the 9th and take 9 and 10 with the thumb when both are white or both black.
  - C example: C2 + D3+E3 under the thumb.
- **Three-note Type A bass shell (1-3-7):** fits any hand. The bass sits fairly high.
  - C examples: Dm7 D3-F3-C4, G7 G2-B2-F3, Cmaj7 C3-E3-B3.
- **Three-note Type B bass shell (1-7-10):** needs a big hand. A sus chord rarely fits.
  - C examples: Dm7 D2-C3-F3, G7 G2-F3-B3, Cmaj7 C2-B2-E3.
- **Substitutes for the root:**
  - The 5th: lower reach in Type A, avoids the stretch in Type B.
  - The tritone, on dominants: a built-in tritone sub. C example: G7 → D♭2-F3-B3.
- **Not simultaneous:** play the bass first, then the essential tones *on* the beat, with or without pedal. Or omit the
  bass at medium and fast tempos.
- **Separating bass and essentials** (ballads and comping):
  - Bass on the strong beats, which gives a "pseudo-stride".
  - Or the shells on the strong beats, which gives syncopation and anticipation.
- **Bill Evans style:** essentials first, bass after, with syncopated comping. His reference recording is "All the
  Things You Are / Midnight Mood" on *Alone*.
- **One essential tone + one colour tone**, still with the bass.
  - C example: Dm7 D3-F3-E4? (**verify** his exact shapes in Ex. B.12).

**Rootless voicings:**
- The shell plus colour tones; Type A has the 3rd on the bottom, Type B the 7th.
- Colour-tone rules:
  - 5th and 9th fit any chord.
  - 13th fits major and dominant chords.
  - 11th fits chords with a minor 3rd.
  - The root is possible above the shell.
  - On diminished chords, any note of the whole-half scale.
  - Altered tones on dominants: ♭9, ♯9, ♭13, ♯11.
- **Avoid stacks of thirds.** On maj and dominant chords, the 13th replaces the 5th. On m7, a stack is accepted, but
  the 11th can replace the 5th.
  - C examples: G7 A = B3-E4-F4-A4 (3-13-7-9); Dm7 A = F3-A3-C4-E4 (stack, accepted).

**Combining the hands:**
- A bass shell or rootless voicing in the LH, with chords in the RH that frame the melody and **avoid doubling**.
- RH harmonization options:
  - One note under the melody, or two notes under it.
  - An **open octave** over the melody, or an octave filled with 1, 2 or 3 notes ("shout chorus style").
  - C example: melody G4 over an LH shell C3-E3-B3. RH: G4+D4; G4+D4+A3; G5+G4; G5+D5+G4 …
- Thick RH chords over only a low bass sound empty, because the essentials are missing from C3–G4.

**Practice (Ex. B.16):** the same chord in every register: bass alone → two-note shell → Type A bass shell → Type B bass
shell → Type A rootless → Type B rootless. The intervals change with the register.

### A3. Left-hand patterns for solo piano

#### Stride (PSJP Ch. 1–4; "7 Secrets of Stride Piano")

Stride is bass on 1 and 3 and a chord on 2 and 4, with the melody in the RH. Stride is improvised; ragtime is written.

"7 Secrets of Stride Piano" (TNm1i71s4is), from its chapter list:
1. **Take breaks:** the LH pauses while the RH cascades.
2. **Walking 10ths.** If you can't reach a 10th, see Ch. B. Practice recommendation: **a whole tune in 10ths**.
3. **Variety in the pattern:**
   - "Low-high-high-low", also called the **Peace Piece pattern** after Bill Evans.
   - **3 + 3 + 2** grouping.
   - Rhythmic variety: the chord placed earlier (demonstrated on Ain't Misbehavin').
   - **Anticipate the bass.**
   - **Anticipate bass and chord.**
   - **Stride in 3 against 4**, and in 5 against 4.
4. **Pedaling, four ways:** none; all four beats; downbeat → offbeat; offbeat → downbeat.
5. **Stride as a ballad.** At fast tempos, simplify.
6. **Feel rather than sight:**
   - A blindfold exercise.
   - Trade between thumb and pinky.
   - Arm placement.
7. **RH chord-melody:** ragtime configurations. At slower tempos the RH completes the LH chords.

"7 Tips to Modernize Your Stride" (with JazzMemes, f3MuTZ8pLEQ):
- fuller voicings and **10ths**
- **walking 10ths**
- **lead-ins**
- **different stride patterns**
- **anticipations**
- pedaling
- **stride-style comping**

Stride variations named in the book's review and summary:
- the **Peace Piece pattern**
- the **"Freddie Green-style"** pattern
- **reversed stride**
- **skip beats**
- **anticipations**
- colour tones in bass and chord

**Procedures I inferred** (verify every row against the book and the videos):

| Variation | Procedure in C (my reading) |
|---|---|
| Walking 10ths | Each beat a 10th (bass plus a 10th above), moving scalewise or chromatically to the next chord. C example: C2+E3 → D2+F3 → E2+G3 → F2+A3 (C6 to F). Teddy Wilson / Hank Jones style. |
| Peace Piece pattern ("low-high-high-low") | Beat 1 bass, beats 2 and 3 chord, beat 4 bass, which then walks or approaches the next downbeat. |

| Skip beat | Leave one beat of the pattern out, often to make room for an RH fill. |
| Freddie Green style | A short chord on all four beats in the LH, with a low root or 10th in the voicing. |
| Stride 3:4 | A three-beat cell (bass-chord-chord) repeated across 4/4 bars, which realigns after three bars. |

#### Stride-style comping (PSJP Ch. 5–6; PianoWithJonny summary)

The LH still covers bass and chord, but spreads them syncopated rather than on every beat.

**Three starting patterns:**
- **Charleston:** 1 and the "and" of 2.
- **Reverse Charleston:** the "and" of 1, then 3.
- **Red Garland pattern:** chords on the offbeats with anticipations, after the coursepack figure (verify).

**Next steps:**
- **Bass fills**, which create an LH–RH "conversation".
- Alternate between patterns.
- Mix long and short comps.
- **Push-offs:** repeat a chord on a downbeat and the following offbeat. "The Comping Variation Every Pianist Should
  Master" (l-x_5vDpglY) uses the push-off to embellish the **Count Basie rhythm**.

**Related videos:**
- "Lead-ins Could Be the Comping Device You're Missing" (faprE9by2bI): a chord on the "and" of 3 or on 4 *leads in* to
  the downbeat (Oscar Peterson, "Easy Does It").
- "Comping Basics: Beat 3 Charleston" (__ugekzPa1w): for call-and-response textures ("Autumn Leaves", "Moanin'",
  "In a Mellow Tone").
- "Accent Comping" (OnhS3jwnR8g): a coordination device, demonstrated on "Yardbird Suite" and "Lullaby of Birdland".
- "All the Comping Patterns I Know Names For" (DUg9h8TdXx0): Red Garland, McCoy Tyner, James P. Johnson, Cedar Walton.

#### Basslines in the left hand (PSJP Ch. 7)

- **Bass in 2:** root on 1, 5th or 3rd on 3; relaxed. **Bass in 4:** root on 1, stepwise by scale; driving.
  - C examples: in 2, C2 (1) G1 (3) → F2 (1) C2 (3). In 4 toward F: C2 D2 E2 E♭2.
- **"6 Types of Basslines"** (fZoNqE51_oY): walking, **march**, in 2, **broken**, **pedal point**, and a sixth
  (verify).
- "Playing Basslines on Piano: Six Common Mistakes" (gkhY9-X9vEQ).
- "Basslines 101" with Danny Ziemann (w7BqTkZmWOU): mistakes, accenting 2 and 4, in 2, in 4, pedal points.
- **"Fix Square Rhythm by Moving the Bass Notes"** (OmXMsINYcmw): in straight-8th, groove or ECM pieces, move bass notes
  off the downbeat for variety.

**The RH over a bassline** ("Solo Piano: 3 Ways to Add Harmony in the Right Hand", ejAwb7ydYyQ):
- **melodic:** imply the chords in the line
- **shuttle:** the RH alternates melody and comping hits
- **drape:** the melody stays on top while the RH reaches down to hold mid-register chord tones

### A4. Leaving out the bass: the "thickened line" (PSJP Ch. 8; PianoWithJonny)

**Tonal melody harmonization:**
- **Closed position ("Shearing")**: five notes spanning an octave. The RH plays four and the LH doubles the melody an
  octave lower.
  - C example: melody E5 on C6 → RH E5-C5-A4-G4, LH E4.
- **Drop 2:** four notes within about a 10th. The RH plays three and the LH one. Lighter and more modern.
  - C example: RH E5-A4-G4, LH C4.
- The LH adds ghost notes, slides, turns and scoops, like a singer or a horn.

**Modal melody harmonization:**
- **So What:** three 4ths plus a major 3rd on top. C example: Dm7 → E3-A3-D4-G4-B4.
- **Quartal:** 4–5 notes in 4ths.
- Demonstration on "Danny Boy".

**Ladders and deep dives:**
- **"6 Levels of Closed-Position (Shearing) Voicings"** (Fq5F_GahFfo), which is itself a ladder:
  1. basics
  2. ornamentation (grace notes, ghost notes, turns)
  3. harmonizing a jazz melody ("Bye Bye Blackbird")
  4. **planing**
  5. **tonicization**
  6. octatonic / parallel, and diatonic passing chords
- "Deep Dive on Locked Hands Comping" (JfRTBqrK48U, "Alone Together"):
  - articulating repeated chords
  - **sidesteps**
  - tonicization
  - for solo piano: shared hands

  - octaves plus one note

- "Planing, Closed-Position, and Drop Two: Three Ways to Harmonize a Melody".

  hands.

### A5. Shared-hands voicings (PSJP Ch. 9–16; "Pro-Level Voicings for Solo Piano Jazz Ballads", ExcZLksoxoA)

**Roles:** the RH melody, the LH bass, and both hands share the mid-register chord.

**Fingers:**
- The RH pinky, ring and middle hold the melody.
- The LH pinky, ring and middle hold the bass.
- The **thumbs and index fingers** share the chord.

**Guidelines:**
1. Include the 3rd and 7th.
2. Don't double.
3. Space the notes fairly evenly.
4. Avoid minor 9ths (a note a ♭9 above a lower one).

**Video chapters:** the guidelines; a *Misty* example; the **corner thumb**; voice leading; demonstrations watching the
notes and the hands. The same video then sets the voicings in four styles: the repeated-quarter-note ballad,
stop-start rubato, a swing setting, and ECM bossa.

C example (mine, verify the spacing taste):
- Cmaj7 with melody E5: LH C2-G2-E3, RH B3-D4 + melody E5 (thumbs and index on E3 / B3).
- A7♭9 with melody C♯5: LH A1-E2-G2, RH C♯3? (keep the 3rd under the melody, avoid ♭9 against the root octave rules).

Related:
- "The Voicing Magic of Interlocking Hands" (rmjri7-g4GE).
- "Learning Big, Beautiful Ballad Voicings: Where to Start" (6rXHC0l02-E, "Easy to Love").
- Voicings 101 #18 "Shared Hands Voicing Basics" and #19 "Performing with Shared Hands Voicings".

Dream Solo on Autumn Leaves"; JPF B1 U5 "Sample First Page of a Dream Solo".

**MTNA 2024 evaluation framework.** He separates performance from creation.
- **Phrasing:** listen for real phrases with beginnings and endings, variety of rhythmic units, varied beginnings and
  endings, and relation to the form.
  - Scale: (Monotonous) – **Organized** – **Varied** – (Random). The two ends are unacceptable.
  - Fixes: listen proactively (the inner ear); phrase patterns (play one / rest one, play two / rest two, sentence,
    period); phrases with pickups; sing the improvisations.
- **Note choice:** matching the chords, tension and resolution, a stylistic amount of chromaticism.
  - Scale: bland – appropriate yet colourful – arbitrary.
  - Fixes: listen reactively; arpeggios; arpeggios with chromatic neighbours; fill arpeggios in with scales.
- **Shape:** a mix of leaps and steps, changes of direction, ornaments, span.
  - Fixes: draw shapes, sing phrases, hand shapes, "overuse" ornaments.
- **Harmony:** cadences, borrowed and secondary chords, modulations.
- **Performance:** balance, pedal, shaping, timing and pauses.

## Part C: build plan

### Ground rules

These come from the owner's standing decisions:
- **Melodies** must be public domain worldwide (STANDARDS_LEGAL Tier 1A) or original. Chord charts are fine.
  - Use the original heads in `src/content/heads.ts`: Corner Bench, Long Way Round, Back Porch Blues, Night Ferry,
    Quiet Hours.
  - Add PD heads (Indiana, Love Me or Leave Me, Mean to Me, My Blue Heaven …) once their transcriptions are verified.
  - Do **not** use Siskind's own tunes ("Evening in Lyon", "Blues for Sammie", "Tone Up", "Hardly, in the Moonlight").
    Write originals that do the same job.
  - His named standards (Misty, Skylark, Chelsea Bridge, The Nearness of You, Autumn Leaves) may appear as **chord
    charts only**.
- **Rubato, then tempo:** every new technique item starts untimed (`rubato.ts`), then a tempo ladder.
- **All 12 keys:** add each new family to `widenFamilies`.
- **One screen, no scroll.** Every new step needs a glyph (FAMILY_GLYPH). It must also pass the six-point "everything
  connects" check: path, map, XP, leagues, level test, workout.
- **Ratings:** give explicit difficulties, like `K_INDEP_DIFF`, so that nothing spills past 1900 and the order is what
  the ladder says, not `KEYS_LATER` × index.
- **Grading:**
  - Bass-shell and shared-hands items: the voice-leading grader (`voiceLead.ts`) with new types, plus register checks
    (essentials between C3 and G4 → feedback "muddy" or "thin").
  - Stride and pattern items: note-for-note, with a beat-role check (bass on the strong beats …).

### C5. Improvisation exercises (into `k-solo` / `jazz` / new small levels)

1. **Drone improvisation** (beginner, 750–850): LH open 5th drone; RH major-scale phrases; a phrase timer; graded on
   phrase organisation (S132 metric) and staying in the scale.
2. **Play one / rest one, play two / rest two** over a form, and between the hands (1150).
3. **Johnaye Kendrick ladder** (voice + keys, 1000–1300): LH bass on the beat, then
   1. sing the 3rds (pitch detection exists)
   2. sing a step-connected line
   3. add a chromatic enclosure
   4. the same with 5ths
   This gives a sung counterpart to jazz-6.
4. **Three Dials** (1450): the app sets the scale, arpeggio and non-chord-tone dials per chorus. The rule cards check
   the proportions.
5. **Pairing (listening) exercise** (1500): each 2-bar phrase must reuse one element of the previous phrase (rhythm or
   contour). The grader compares phrases.
6. **Personalize the melody** (1200): the head with required devices (grace note, ghost note, back-phrasing, a rhythm
   change). This is the MIDI-detectable subset.
7. **Phrase-organisation metric** for all free improv (from the MTNA rubric): rests between phrases, a varied phrase
   start beat, a varied length, and relation to the 4/8-bar form. Report it as *monotonous ↔ organized ↔ varied ↔
   random*. This is a cheap and very "practice-quality" signal.

### C6. Practice method (Settings / Workout / Study)

- **Four-slot session template**: technique → repetition and transposition → tunes → recordings (listening). The daily
  plan fills the slots from due items. The "recordings" slot is a guided listening card. It must be "self-generating":
  an item mastered in slot 2 is pushed into slot 3 on a tune.
- **COREA / 7-step flow for a lick**:
  1. pick
  2. 12 keys (exists)
  3. tag the concept (rhythm / contour / harmony)
  4. new phrase over the same chord
  5. over other chords
  6. overdo it on a tune
  7. tasteful use
  Steps 4–7 can be graded by rule cards that require the tagged concept. Mark the acronym (verify).
- **Tune memorization flow** (Tune): chunk by form → transpose at once (2 keys on day 1) → write the changes from memory
  (chart-dictation topic exists) → sing melody, roots, 3rds → loop listening → perform → the **three lists** (know /
  learning / next). This ties into My songs and the repertoire map.
- **Outlining mode** for any tempo-ladder item: play only the phrase-target notes at the goal tempo first, then fill in.
  It is the Whiteside alternative to slow-to-fast, and should be offered as an option, not a replacement.
- **Self-transcription**: after a free take, the app shows the student's notes; the student marks one idea to keep,
  which feeds the COREA flow.
