# Jazz standards in The 7th Sense: what we can legally use, and how

*Research done 2026-11-10 for ToneTurn / The 7th Sense (commercial PWA, Israeli owner, worldwide users).*
*This is research, not legal advice. Items marked **[lawyer]** need an Israeli and a US/EU IP lawyer to confirm them before we charge money. This builds on `docs/research/MUSIC_SOURCES_LEGAL.md` (2026-11-07), which covers general music sources (Spotify, YouTube, lyrics, tabs and so on). This file covers only jazz standards and lead sheets.*

---

## 0. Bottom line

| Tier | What | Melody? | Lyrics? | Where | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1A** | Tunes that are **public domain worldwide**: published 1930 or earlier, **and** every composer **and lyricist** died in 1955 or earlier | Yes, full lead sheet | Yes | Everywhere | Low (check each song) |
| **1B** | Tunes that are **PD in the US only**: published 1930 or earlier, but an author died after 1955 (Gershwin, Porter, Berlin, Waller, Handy…) | US only, or after a lawyer clears the "rule of the shorter term" for EU/Israel | Same as melody | Chord charts everywhere; melody/lyrics gated | Medium **[lawyer]** |
| **2** | **Chord-only charts** of copyrighted standards (Autumn Leaves, All the Things You Are, Blue Bossa…), written by us, with no melody, no lyrics and no signature riff | No | No | Everywhere | Low–medium (US fairly settled; IL/EU untested) **[lawyer]** |
| **3** | **Our own contrafacts**: new melodies we compose over blues, rhythm changes and standard forms | Yes (ours) | Optional (ours) | Everywhere | Low |
| **4** | **Licensed** melodies and lyrics of copyrighted standards | Yes | Yes | Per licence | Needs money + contracts; later |

**So the owner's "must use standards" goal is mostly reachable without a licence.** Comping, walking, soloing, ear training on progressions and band play-along all run on **changes**, and changes are Tier 2 (already shipped). Sing-along and "play the head" need **melody**, so they run on Tier 1A/1B tunes plus our Tier 3 contrafacts. Real heads of post-1930 standards (Tier 4) wait for a licence.

## 1. Copyright basics

### 1.1 What is protected

| Element | US | EU / Israel | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Melody** | Protected (the core of the musical work) | Protected | A transcription of a PD melody made by someone else (a fake book, Real Book or modern edition) may carry its **own** arrangement copyright, so we copy from original period sheet music or write it out ourselves |
| **Lyrics** | Protected (literary work) | Protected | Need a separate licence (LyricFind/Musixmatch, the publisher, or ACUM for Israeli works) |
| **Chord progression alone** | **Generally not protected.** *Structured Asset Sales v. Sheeran* (2d Cir. 2024) treats a common progression plus harmonic rhythm as basic building blocks; Copyright Office *Compendium (Third)* §802.5(A) | No court case squarely on point; originality tests ("author's own intellectual creation" in the EU, §4 of Israel's 2007 Act) point the same way | Caveat: an original *selection and arrangement* of unprotected elements can be protected, so a chart that also copies a riff, intro line or melody fragment is no longer "chords only" |
| **Riffs / bass lines / signature vamps** | Protected as melody | Protected | Examples: the *Chameleon* bass riff, the *Cantaloupe Island* piano figure, the *Song for My Father* bass line, the *So What* bass call. **Never generate these note for note** |
| **Voicings, comping patterns, walking lines** we generate | Our own expression | Our own | No issue. A quartal "So What voicing" is a technique, not a work |
| **Titles** | Not protected (Compendium §313.4(C)) | Not protected as works | Use them only to identify the tune. Don't imply endorsement. **"Real Book" and "iReal" are trademarks**, so don't name our library after them |
| **Sound recordings** | Separate copyright | Separate | Irrelevant here: our band is generated, and we don't use recordings |

### 1.2 Term rules

| Jurisdiction | Rule for musical works | PD on 1 Jan 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| **US** | Works published before 1978: 95 years from publication | Everything **published 1930 or earlier**. 1931 works follow on 1 Jan 2027, 1932 on 2028, and so on ([Duke CSPD 2026](https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2026/); [2025](https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2025/)) |
| **EU** (Term Directive 2006/116/EC) | Life + 70, counted to the end of the year | Authors who **died in 1955 or earlier** |
| **EU: songs with words** (Directive 2011/77/EU, Art. 1) | A "musical composition with words" runs until 70 years after the death of the **last survivor of composer and lyricist**, when both parts were written for that song | **The trap:** George Gershwin died in 1937, but Ira died in 1983, so *I Got Rhythm* could stay protected in the EU until the end of 2053 |
| **Israel** (Copyright Act 2007) | Life + 70, counted to the end of the year ([ACUM statutes](https://acum.org.il/en/israeli-copyright-law/); [Commons summary](https://c.enwp.org/wiki/Commons:CRT/Israel)) | Authors who died in 1955 or earlier. Whether the EU-style "song with words" rule applies in Israel is unclear **[lawyer]** |
| **Rule of the shorter term** | EU Art. 7: a non-EU work gets no longer than its term in the country of origin. Israel: practitioner guides say foreign works get the **shorter** of the Israeli term and the origin term ([Israeli practitioner summary](https://www.law.co.il/media/knowledge-centers/copyright_2020_israel.pdf); [IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/new-copyright-law-provides-long-awaited-overhaul)) | This could make US works that are now PD in the US **also PD in most of the EU and in Israel**. **Exceptions:** Germany does not apply the rule to US works, because of the 1892 US–Germany treaty (OLG Frankfurt, 2003) ([copyrighthistory.org](https://copyrighthistory.org/cam/commentary/d_1892/d_1892_com_228200818226.html); [overview](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Rule_of_shorter_term)), and member states differ on the details **[lawyer]** |
| **Outliers** | Mexico life + 100; Colombia life + 80; some others longer | A "worldwide" claim realistically means US + life+70 countries. Mexico is a residual risk **[lawyer]** |

**Concrete examples (status in 2026):**

| Tune | Published | Authors (death year) | US | EU/IL by life+70 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Back Home Again in Indiana* | 1917 | Hanley (1942), MacDonald (1935) | PD | PD | **Worldwide**. Its changes also give us *Donna Lee* |
| *I Got Rhythm* | 1930 | G. Gershwin (1937), I. Gershwin (1983) | PD since 2026 | Possibly protected to the end of 2053 (song rule); possibly PD by the shorter-term rule | **US-only** until cleared |
| *Body and Soul* | 1930 | Green (1989), Heyman, Sour, Eyton | PD since 2026 | Protected to about 2059 | **US-only** |
| *St. Louis Blues* | 1914 | W.C. Handy (1958) | PD | PD from 1 Jan 2029 | US-only until 2029 |
| *What Is This Thing Called Love?* | 1929 | Cole Porter (1964), sole author | PD since 2025 | PD from 1 Jan 2035 | US-only until 2035 |
| *Autumn Leaves* | 1945 (*Les feuilles mortes*) | Kosma (1973), Prévert (1977), Mercer (1976) | Protected | Protected | Chord-only (Tier 2) |

## 3. Chord-only charts of copyrighted standards (Tier 2)

**Is it legal?** In the US, very probably yes, for a chart that holds only chord symbols, the form (AABA, 1st/2nd endings) and the title as an identifier. This rests on *Sheeran* (2d Cir. 2024), Compendium §802.5(A) and *Skidmore v. Led Zeppelin* (9th Cir. 2020), cited in `MUSIC_SOURCES_LEGAL.md` §2.1. Israel and the EU have no case law. The same originality reasoning should apply, but a test case is possible. **[lawyer]**

**How the market does it:**

| Product | Approach | Lesson for us |
|---|---|---|
| **iReal Pro** | Chord charts only. Its store listing says it can't include melodies or lyrics because of copyright. The app was first called *iReal Book* and was renamed after a copyright claim ([Symposium review](https://symposium.music.org/volume-63-no-2/technology-and-online-resource-reviews/ireal-pro-a-revolution-in-jazz-education-practice-tools); [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/223657/ireal-pro-for-mac-review-a-harmonious-practice-tool-for-musicians.html)). Content comes from **user-made charts on its forums** ("Jazz 1460" and so on), downloaded for free ([iReal Pro: get songs from the forums](https://www.irealpro.com/learn/get-songs-from-the-forums); [forum: no melody](https://forums.irealpro.com/threads/adding-melodies-to-charts.8893)) | A decade-plus of chord-only charts under real titles, at scale, with no reported publisher suit. **But** the company keeps the forum separate from what it ships, and avoided the "Real Book" name |
| **Band-in-a-Box** (PG Music) | Users type their own changes. PG Music ships chord-only demo songs and its own melodies | Chord entry by the user is not an issue |
| **Real Book** | The 1970s Real Books were unlicensed. Hal Leonard's *Sixth Edition* (2004) is the licensed version, with melodies and royalties paid ([Hal Leonard](https://www.halleonard.com/product/240221/the-real-book-volume-i-sixth-edition)) | Melodies need a licence; changes are what everyone shares |
| **Wikifonia** | User lead sheets **with melody**, licensed through collecting societies. It shut down on 25 Dec 2013 when that licence could not be extended ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikifonia)) | Melody-bearing UGC depends on a licence; chord-only does not |
| **Worship/pop chord sites** | PraiseCharts and OnSong license chord charts from publishers, and say "free" chord sites are often unauthorised ([PraiseCharts](https://help.praisecharts.com/article/198-why-pay-for-chord-charts-when-they-are-free-online)) | Pop/worship publishers do assert rights over chord charts (usually because the charts include lyrics). This is a residual risk, smaller for instrumental jazz changes |

**Risks, in order:**
1. **Copying someone else's chart.** Importing iReal forum charts, Real Book changes or another app's data copies *their* choices (reharms, layout). In the EU it may also infringe the **sui generis database right** (Directive 96/9/EC) if we take a substantial part of a collection. **We write each chart ourselves**, from our own ear and from common knowledge, and keep a note of how each one was made.
2. **Slipping in melody**: an intro line, a written-out riff, a "head" bass line, a melodic hint in the voicing top note that traces the tune. Our band must not voice the melody on top. (This is a real temptation for "guess the tune" games.)
3. **Lyrics** in titles or hints, such as quoting lyric lines in exercise copy.
4. **IL/EU uncertainty** (no case law), and **ACUM**. ACUM's internet licence lists "texts, notes **and chords**" ([ACUM FAQ](https://acum.org.il/en/faq/)), so for **Israeli** songs ACUM may claim that chord charts need a licence. Avoid Israeli copyrighted songs in Tier 2 until ACUM is asked. **[lawyer]**
5. **Trademarks**: "Real Book", "iReal", "Fake Book" as product names. Describe ours as "standards' changes".

**Safe practice (what we already mostly do):**
- Real titles are fine as identifiers ("Autumn Leaves – the changes"). Add composer credit text ("changes of a tune by J. Kosma") as a courtesy. Never use album art.
- No melody, no lyrics, no signature riff. Our own voicings, comping and walking lines.
- Our own harmonic choices (a standard common-practice version), written by us.
- User charts (lead-sheet import) stay **private by default**. If users can later share charts: chords only, terms that make users responsible, a notice-and-takedown process and a registered **DMCA agent** (US Copyright Office, small fee). For EU users, follow the DSA notice-and-action rules. **[lawyer]**
- Generic titles ("Minor ii–V cycle in G, AABA") are a fallback that removes almost all residual risk, but they cost the "real standards" value. Recommendation: keep real titles for changes.

## 4. Original contrafacts (Tier 3)

- **Legal status:** A new melody over existing changes is a **new, original work owned by its author** (us). The changes are free to use (§3). This is how bebop built its repertoire: *Donna Lee* (Indiana), *Ornithology* (How High the Moon), *Hot House* (What Is This Thing Called Love), *Anthropology* and *Oleo* (I Got Rhythm), *Groovin' High* (Whispering), *Evidence* (Just You, Just Me), *In Walked Bud* (Blue Skies), *Bird of Paradise* (All the Things You Are). Composers claimed credit and royalties for the new heads ([Jazz Piano Site](https://www.thejazzpianosite.com/jazz-piano-lessons/jazz-chord-progressions/contrafacts/); [JazzFuel](https://jazzfuel.com/?p=97862)). We found no reported lawsuit over a contrafact that used only the changes.
- **Rules for ours:** (a) composed fresh, without quoting or paraphrasing the original head; (b) titles of our own (don't call it "Autumn Leaves 2"; "on the changes of…" in the description is fine); (c) if AI tools help, a human composes or curates, and we keep drafts, since copyright ownership of AI-only output is weak in the US; (d) register the copyright of a batch later if they become a product asset (cheap in the US; ACUM membership for Israel).
- **Fit:** contrafacts give us **real heads** for sing-along, "learn the head", melody dictation and call-and-response on *exactly* the forms students need (blues, rhythm changes, Indiana, ATTYA-type, minor ii–V cycle, Giant Steps cycle), with no licence and no geo-gating.

## 5. Licensing real lead sheets later (Tier 4)

| Route | What it gives | Reality for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Hal Leonard** (owned by Muse Group since 2023, which also owns MuseScore and Ultimate Guitar) | The largest print catalogue (Real Book 6th ed., Sheet Music Direct, ArrangeMe). It has a **Digital Licensing** team for "music tech partners" ([MMR, 2022](https://mmrmagazine.com/site/news/news-people/hal-leonard-expands-digital-licensing-with-creation-of-new-role/); [publishing programs](https://www.halleonard.com/licensing/publishingPrograms.jsp)) | **No public API** or self-serve programme found. Bespoke deal. Muse Group owns competing apps |
| **Publishers directly**: Concord (Gershwin via Warner Chappell, Rodgers & Hammerstein via Concord), Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, UMPG, plus small jazz publishers and composer estates | **Print/display** rights for melody (+ lyrics) per song | Typical print royalty is **about 10–12.5% of retail per copy** for sheet music ([Passman summary](https://www.slideshare.net/EricGriffin3/survey-passman-chap-17); [example licence](https://www.acappella.org/get-the-music/licensing/print-license/)). For apps, expect per-display or revenue-share terms plus **advances / minimum guarantees**. Many contracts. Feasible only song by song for a handful of key tunes, or once funded |
| **Performance (PROs)**: ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, GMR (US); ACUM (Israel); PRS, GEMA, SACEM and others | Needed if the app **plays a copyrighted melody** (a public performance). Not needed for changes only | BMI digital licences start at **$350/year** minimum ([BMI](https://www.bmi.com/licensing/website-mobile-app)). Licences are territorial, so worldwide means many societies. ACUM has internet tariffs ([ACUM](https://acum.org.il/en/tariff-internet-commercial-services/)) |
| **Mechanical / reproduction** (US MLC blanket, HFA) | For distributing recordings of works | An in-app generated rendition of a melody is a grey zone **[lawyer]** |
| **Sync** | For music fixed to video | Only if we make lesson videos with copyrighted tunes |
| **Musicnotes / Sheet Music Direct** | Sell licensed digital sheet music ([Musicnotes publishers](https://musicnotes.com/publishers)) | Retail only, no catalogue licence for third-party apps. A possible **affiliate link-out**: "buy the lead sheet", and the user imports it with our MusicXML/iReal import for private use |
| **Soundslice** | Embedding/notation tech. Its own policy says printed arrangements need a print licence, even your own ([content policy](https://soundslice.com/help/en/store-selling/policies/153/content-policy)) | It confirms the rule; it is not a licence source |
| **Precedents**: Ultimate Guitar (HFA + direct deals), Songsterr, flowkey, Simply Piano | Consumer apps can license catalogues | Done at scale, with funding and lawyers. Not a pre-revenue step |

**Practical path:** (1) launch on Tiers 1–3; (2) add **"bring your own lead sheet"** (already built: MusicXML, iReal and text import) as private, user-owned use; (3) once there is revenue, approach Hal Leonard Digital Licensing and one or two jazz-focused publishers or estates for ~20 heads, with ACUM for Israel.

## 8. Recommendation

**Strategy**
1. **Tier 2 is the backbone.** Keep and grow chord-only standards under real titles, written by us, for comping, walking, soloing, ear training, progression puzzles and band play-along. Add a header rule to `standards.ts`: no melody, no riff, own write-up, provenance noted.
2. **Tier 1A for "real heads"** (melody, sing-along, head-learning, melody dictation): transcribe ~20–30 worldwide-PD tunes from period sheet music, writing our own clean lead sheets (stored with source + PD reasoning per tune: publication year and every author's death year).
3. **Tier 3 contrafacts** to fill the gaps where 1A has no tune on a key form (rhythm changes, minor blues, ATTYA-type, modal, Coltrane): about one original head per form, with our own titles.
4. **Tier 1B** (Gershwin, Porter, Handy, Berlin…): ship their **changes** now (Tier 2 rules). Ship **melodies/lyrics** only after a lawyer rules on the shorter-term question for Israel and the EU. Until then gate them by region (US allow-list), or wait for the year each one turns PD. Keep a `pdWorldwideFrom` year on each tune so they unlock automatically.
5. **Tier 4 later:** a licence for ~20 famous heads after revenue (Hal Leonard Digital Licensing, plus ACUM for Israel). Until then, "bring your own lead sheet" stays private.
6. **Before charging money:** a one-time lawyer review (Israel + US/EU) of: chords-only in IL/EU; the shorter-term rule and the song-with-words rule for 1B; ACUM's position on chord charts; UGC sharing terms. Also register a DMCA agent.
