Written 2026-11-15. Trigger: the owner watched a Larry Goldings lecture on Open Studio about **common-tone reharmonization**: hold one note and harmonize it as a different chord degree each time. The owner also noted that **chromatic mediants** and **modal interchange** were never raised in any of our audits. This file looks for every gap of that kind across reharmonization and color harmony.

Status words used below:
- **EXISTS**: built, with ids and depth.
- **SHALLOW**: built in part. The entry says what is missing: performed, 12 keys, instruments, or use in tunes.
- **MISSING**: nothing in the app.

Every note spelling in this file was worked out degree by degree; the tables list the chord tones. Ratings use the app's 700–1900 scale.

### A.2 The lecture the owner watched (common-tone reharm)

I found **no public page** that names this lecture or gives its handout. Its most likely home is Jazz Organ Essentials lesson 4, "Chord Substitutions and Sequences", or an Open Studio live session. Both are **unverified**: Open Studio lesson pages need a login. The method below comes from the owner's description and from standard pedagogy, not from a Goldings transcript.

**Concept G-CT: one note, many chords** (unverified as a named Goldings exercise; the procedure is standard and checked below)

- **Procedure.** Pick a melody note (F). Play it on top and keep it there. Under it, play chords in which F is, in turn, each chord degree. Move up the ladder: triads, then 7th chords, then drop 2 voicings with F as the top voice, then four bars where each new chord is approached by voice leading.
- **Progression use.** The held note becomes a pedal in the soprano while the harmony moves. This is the basis of reharmonizing a long melody note, a fermata, a tag or an intro.
- **Instruments.** Piano and organ (F held in the right hand, and on organ literally sustained). Guitar: F on string 1 (fret 1) or string 2 (fret 6) with drop 2 shapes under it on strings 2–4. Bass: plays the roots under a held F, which works as a duo exercise.

**F held, each degree.** Each row gives the chord, its tones in spelled order, and F's role. For the drop 2 column, take the close voicing with F on top and drop the second voice from the top an octave.

**Concept G-ORG: organ texture** (verified as course topics: G1, G2, G7)
- Left-hand walking bass with the pedal doubling on beats 1 and 3.
- Right-hand pads on the upper manual: a 3- or 4-note voicing held while one inner voice moves (counter melody, lesson 3).
- "Textures and transparency" (lesson 6): thin the voicing under a soloist and thicken it at section changes.
- **In C:** LH walks C–D–E–G under C6. The RH holds E G A and moves the inner G → G♯ → A (a counter line).
- Instruments: organ, also piano with the Wurlitzer and organ voices already in the app.

**Concept G-VOIC: one chord, many voicings for one emotion** (verified, G6)
- Play the same chord as a triad, a 7th chord, a cluster and a drop 2, and choose the one that fits the mood.
- **In C:** Cmaj7 as C E G (triad), E G B D (rootless), B C E G (cluster), G C E B (drop 2 of C E G B).

**Concept G-JAR: the inevitable answer** (verified, G8)
- Given a 4-bar phrase, write a 4-bar answer with unlimited time. Then do the same faster, then improvise it.
- This maps to a "compose then improvise" ladder. It is a composition exercise, not a harmony drill.

### B.6 Why the earlier audits missed these

All three audits measured depth **from inside the app's existing lists**: devices already in `reharm.ts`, families already in the catalogue. AUDIT-CONTENT-THEORY checked the classical chromatic list (Neapolitan, aug 6th, secondary leading-note), and the jazz audits checked the Barry Harris and bebop lists. Nobody started from an external syllabus for **color harmony** (Berklee reharmonization, Felts, the Levine "Jazz Theory" reharm chapters, the Open Studio harmony courses). That is the fix for the future: every audit opens with an external checklist first (this Part B), then maps it to the app.

## Part C: The build plan

Conventions: every performed item in **all 12 keys** (cyc4), **rubato first, then a tempo ladder**, timing cue always, each new topic gets a glyph (`FAMILY_GLYPH`/`TOPIC_GLYPH`), each family feeds path / map / XP / leagues / level test / workout. Compact questions, one-line feedback, "Why?" only on a wrong answer. Each step opens with a gate page (text, refresher, Play).

Common tones, checked:
- C (C E G) and E (E G♯ B): common E.
- C and A♭ (A♭ C E♭): common C.

- C and E♭ (E♭ G B♭): common G.

## 1. Two families of reharm

- **Functional:** the new chord does the old chord's job (pull or rest). Secondary dominants, tritone sub, added ii–V, backdoor, passing diminished.
- **Color:** the new chord changes the light, not the direction. Common tone, chromatic mediants, modal interchange, pedal point, planing, side-slip.

A reharm is valid when the melody note is a chord tone or an available tension of the new chord (never an avoid note). It is good when the voices move smoothly.

## 2. One note, many chords (common-tone reharm)

Hold the melody note; change what it means.

| Note F is… | Chord | Tones |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | F, Fm, Fmaj7, Fm6 | F A C · F A♭ C · F A C E · F A♭ C D |
| ♭3 | Dm7 | D F A C |
| 3 | D♭maj7 | D♭ F A♭ C |
| 5 | B♭maj7, B♭m6 | B♭ D F A · B♭ D♭ F G |
| 6 / 13 | A♭6, A♭13 | A♭ C E♭ F · A♭ C E♭ G♭ + F |
| ♭7 | G7, Gm7, Gø7 | G B D F · G B♭ D F · G B♭ D♭ F |
| maj7 | G♭maj7 | G♭ B♭ D♭ F |
| 9 | E♭9 | E♭ G B♭ D♭ + F |
| ♭9 | E7♭9 | E G♯ B D F |
| 11 | C7sus4 | C F G B♭ |
| ♭13 | A7♭13 | A C♯ E G F |
| ♯9, ♯11 | D7♯9, B7♯11 | spelled E♯ (= F). In teaching use the C examples: A7♯9 (B♯ = C), G♭7♯11 (C) |

Drop 2 with the note on top: write the close voicing under the note, then drop the second voice from the top an octave. G7 under F: close G B D F → drop 2 D G B F. D♭maj7 under F: A♭ C D♭ F → D♭ A♭ C F.

**The common-tone diminished.** C6 – C°7 – C6: C and A stay, E and G move a half step (E → E♭, G → G♭). (Same as the Barry Harris auxiliary diminished, BARRY-HARRIS-THEORY.)

## 3. Chromatic mediants

Two major (or two minor) chords a 3rd apart, with one common tone:

| From C | Mediant | Tones | Common tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| up M3 | E | E G♯ B | E |
| down M3 | A♭ | A♭ C E♭ | C |
| down m3 | A | A C♯ E | E |
| up m3 | E♭ | E♭ G B♭ | G |

**Doubly chromatic:** C ↔ A♭m (A♭ C♭ E♭) and C ↔ E♭m (E♭ G♭ B♭) share no tone. It is the most distant sound in the system.

**Coltrane.** C – A♭ – E are major-third mediants of one another; their roots spell the augmented triad C E G♯/A♭.

**Lens (brief).** P = parallel (C ↔ Cm), R = relative (C ↔ Am), L = leading-tone exchange (C ↔ Em). Alternating P and L gives the hexatonic cycle C – Cm – A♭ – A♭m – E – Em (notes C E♭ E G A♭ B). Alternating P and R gives the octatonic cycle C – Am – A – F♯m – F♯ – E♭m – E♭ – Cm. Its roots C A F♯ E♭ are the minor-third cycle of the family of dominants.

## 4. Modal interchange (borrowed chords)

Chords from the parallel minor (or another mode on the same tonic) used in major.

| Chord in C | Tones | From | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| iv / ivm6 / ivm7 | F A♭ C / + D / + E♭ | Aeolian / Dorian minor | PD, minor plagal |
| iiø7 | D F A♭ C | Aeolian | PD (into V7♭9) |
| ♭IIImaj7 | E♭ G B♭ D | Aeolian | T color |
| ♭VImaj7 | A♭ C E♭ G | Aeolian | PD or deceptive target |
| ♭VII7 | B♭ D F A♭ | Aeolian / Mixolydian | D (backdoor) |
| v m7 | G B♭ D F | Mixolydian | weak D, a ii of IV |
| ♭IImaj7 | D♭ F A♭ C | Phrygian | D substitute / plagal color |

**Minor plagal** = any of iv, ivm6, ♭VI, ♭VII7, ♭IImaj7 going straight to I. In minor the borrowing reverses: Picardy I, IV7 (Dorian), ii m7.

## 5. Other devices (one line each)

- **Pedal point:** the bass holds (tonic or dominant) while the chords above change: Dm7/G – F/G – A♭/G – G7.
- **Planing:** one shape in parallel. Diatonic planing keeps the key; exact planing (constant structure) keeps the quality.
- **Side-slip:** a half step out and back on purpose (Dm7 → E♭m7 → Dm7).
- **Chromatic approach chord:** the target's voicing a half step above or below just before it (D♭maj7 → Cmaj7).
- **Deceptive (jazz):** V7 to anything but I: ♭VImaj7, iii, IV, V/ii.
- **Line cliché:** one voice moves chromatically inside a held chord (Cm – Cm(maj7) – Cm7 – Cm6).
- **Counter-line:** the melody holds; the bass walks by step; each bass note renames the chord (C, Cmaj7/B, C7/B♭, Am7, A♭maj7, G7sus4 under a held C).
- **Upper structure / slash:** a triad over a bass (D/C = Cmaj7♯11 sound; F/G = G9sus4).
- **Sus reharm:** ii–V → V7sus4 (F/G).

**(verify)** The Larry Goldings presentation of "one note, many chords" (Open Studio). Align the wording when the owner shares the handout.
