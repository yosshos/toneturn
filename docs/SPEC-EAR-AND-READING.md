# By ear and from the page: two channels in every course (draft for the owner, 2026-11-22)

**What the owner said (2026-11-22):**
- "My ear is excellent; learning a lick by ear is easy for me, but I am not very skilled at reading."
- The skill map has "by ear" and "reading". If we don't define the split now, neither can be measured properly.
- The Ear course is almost all ear; the piano course always puts notation in front of me.
- His seed idea: learn lick A from the page and lick B from the page, then hear a phrase that combines them (plus something new) and play it back. It's a challenge that trains both.
- His request: a thorough, deep and creative design.

**A correction to my own earlier line:** "a lick learned by ear is worth more" is wrong. What is worth more is work in **your weaker channel**. For the owner that is reading; for a classically trained reader it is the ear.

## 1. The core idea: the channel belongs to the take, not to the exercise

**Today:**
- "By ear" = the families k-ear / k-phrase, and "reading" = k-read (courseSections.ts).
- Every other exercise is always shown as notation, and nobody measures *how* it was learned.
- Licks already have a by-ear mode: notation hidden until revealed (licks.ts `ByEar`, with `byEarCredit`).

**From now on, every take records the channel it was played through:**

| Channel | What the player gets | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Page (sight)** | notation, no audio before the first take, material not seen before | reading: sight-reading |
| **Page (known)** | notation, material already played | technique, not reading: after a few takes the eyes no longer read |
| **Ear (fresh)** | audio only, notation hidden, material not heard before | playing by ear: hear it, find it, play it |
| **Ear (known)** | audio only, material already played | aural memory |
| **Concept** | only a name: "3rds along E♭ major", "lick 2 into the triads" | understanding: playing from a concept, as professionals practise |
| **Memory** | nothing at all, the player plays what they know | internalised |

**The measurement rule that follows (the important part):**
- **Reading is measured only on "page, sight" takes.** An exercise played 20 times with the notes on screen is technique, not reading.
- **By ear is measured only on "ear, fresh" takes.**
- **Technique** (clean at the target tempo) is measured in every channel.
- **"Concept" and "memory"** feed a new indicator, "in the fingers" (what the player knows without help). It fits "in my language" for licks.

## 2. Which channel, when: matched to the player

### 2.1 The player's channel profile

Two numbers, each from 0 to 1, built from the measured takes above:
- **Ear:** accuracy on a first take by ear, and the number of tries until it is right.
- **Reading:** accuracy and tempo on a first sight take.

**Where they come from:**
- the onboarding question (self-report: "I learn better by ear / from notes / both");
- a short check in the level test: one phrase by ear and one from the page;
- every take after that.

**What it does:** it decides the default presentation and the mix (2.2), and it shows on the skill map as two separate axes, by ear and reading, alongside the course's sections.

### 2.2 The rule: learn in the strong channel, practise the weak one

Every new item (lick, exercise, piece) goes through three steps:
1. **Learn:** in the strong channel, so learning is quick and pleasant. For the owner, by ear.
2. **Bridge:** the other channel joins. Someone who learned by ear sees the notation playing along with their own take ("this is what you played"). Someone who learned from the page closes their eyes: the notation fades.
3. **Practise:** from then on the coach rotates channels by need:
   - **about 70%** in the strong channel;
   - **about 20%** in the weak one;
   - **about 10%** in concept or memory.

   These shares are tilted by the gap between the two numbers in the profile: the bigger the gap, the more the weak channel gets.

### 2.3 Fading the notation (for known material)

An item already played clean no longer needs full notation. The coach thins it in four steps:
1. noteheads;
2. rhythm only;
3. a name or chord symbols only;
4. nothing (memory).

Fading drops a step after the item closes. It returns a step after a miss.
- **For a weak reader**, the order reverses on new material: first sound and then notation ("read along"), so the eye links sound to symbol.

## 3. In each place

### 3.1 Technique (where it is missing most)

- **Concept by default** for known patterns: "the scale in 3rds" with no notation. The notation is one tap away. This is the real world of technique: professionals don't read a scale.
- **Hear and continue (ear):** the coach plays the first two segments of the pattern (for example triads on degrees 1 and 2), and the player continues the pattern along the whole scale by ear.
- **Transpose by ear:** the coach plays a pattern or lick in E♭, and the player plays it in A♭ without notation.
- **Call and response in the key session:** a one-bar motif from the coach, an answer from the player, inside the key's harmony. This links to the "by ear" families.
- **Sight dose:** once a day the coach puts a new short exercise in the key of the day (from the reading family, generated fresh) in front of the player, as a sight take. It is short, measured, and counts only toward reading.

### 3.2 Licks (the owner's seed, expanded)

**Each lick has the two learning modes** (from the page / by ear) plus the 9 graded "steps for the ear".

**The new challenge, "the combined sentence" (the owner's idea):**
1. You learned lick A and lick B, in any channel.
2. The app plays a **new** sentence: A + B + a link note or a short new element, over the right chord.
3. If A and B were learned from the page, the sentence comes **by ear**. If they were learned by ear, the sentence comes **from the page**.
4. You play it back. The debrief shows which part you recognised (A, B) and which part was new.

This trains **chunking**, the way real transcription works: you recognise words you know inside a new sentence. And it crosses the channel deliberately.

**Variations by level:**
- A and B in a new key;
- A displaced in rhythm;
- three licks;
- a sentence from the player's own Encore phrases.

**Reverse recognition:** hear a longer passage, then mark where lick A appeared (it can be a tap during play). This is ear recognition of vocabulary, with no playing at all, so it suits the Ear course too.

### 3.3 Repertoire

**Every piece offers both ways to learn the melody:**
- **by ear:** a phrase at a time, play it back, the notation revealed after;
- **from the page:** the piece coach.

The default comes from the profile.

**A standard that is chords only:** comping by ear is possible too: hear the progression with the band, and play the voicings without the chart (the chart can be revealed).

### 3.4 Improvise

- **Trading by ear:** the band "answers" with a phrase, and you reply to its rhythm or motif.
- **The lead sheet itself:** it can be turned off after the progression is known (memory). This connects to the memorising steps in Repertoire.

### 3.5 The Ear course (the other way round)

In the Ear course you answer by tapping. The bridge to playing:
- **"Now play it":** after a correct identification (an interval, a chord, a phrase), an option to play it on an instrument if one is connected.
- **Reading in the Ear course:** the reading exercises that exist (notes, key signatures) stay; the "page, sight" channel measures them the same way.

## 4. The skill map and the level test

- **Two axes (by ear, reading) in every instrument course.** They are computed from takes by channel (§1), not from families. The current families (k-ear, k-phrase, k-read) feed them automatically, because their takes are ear-fresh and page-sight.
- **"In the fingers"** is shown beside technique: the share of closed items that also passed in concept or memory.
- **The level test** gains two short checks: one sentence by ear and one at sight. They place the profile (2.1).

## 5. Reward and motivation

- **XP per take:** the same base everywhere. **The weak channel earns more** (a factor that shrinks as the gap closes). A clean take with no help (memory) earns a small extra.
- **A weekly balance:** "this week: by ear 64%, reading 22%, memory 14%", with the profile's target.
- **No penalty in the strong channel,** and no hiding of it: the goal is growth, not discomfort.

## 6. Data (no schema migration needed)

- **Every take gets** `channel: 'page-sight' | 'page-known' | 'ear-fresh' | 'ear-known' | 'concept' | 'memory'` (`seen` / `heard` are derived from history).
- **Item state gains** `fade` (0–3) and `channels` (best tempo per channel).
- **The player profile gains** `ear`, `reading` and a target mix.
- **Old takes** count as "page-known" (that is what they were), so nothing old is credited to reading by mistake.

## 7. Order of work

1. The channel on every take, the measurement rule, and the two axes on the skill map. This alone fixes the measurement.
2. Licks: the two modes for every lick; the 9 levels as steps for the ear; the combined sentence.
3. Technique: concept by default for known patterns, fading the notation, hear-and-continue, transpose by ear, the daily sight dose.
4. The profile and the rotation (2.2) in the coach and the daily workout; the two checks in the level test.
5. Repertoire and Improvise: melody by ear, chords from memory, trading by ear.

## 8. Questions for the owner

1. **The mix:** about 70/20/10 (strong / weak / memory), tilted by the gap. Is that the right starting point?
2. **Concept by default in technique:** known patterns shown by name only, notation one tap away. Or keep notation on screen until the player turns it off?
3. **The onboarding question:** ask "by ear / from notes / both" when starting a course, or rely only on the measurement (the level test and the first takes)?
