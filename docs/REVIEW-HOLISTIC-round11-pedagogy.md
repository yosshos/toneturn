# Round 11: content and pedagogy review (HOLISTIC-2 v12 + SP v6)

## (1) Verdict: NOT CLEAN (3 major, 0 blockers; none of them touches step 1)

Overall the content is broad, well sequenced and buildable. It covers listen, sing, play, memory, transfer, the story ladder, accompaniment, the lab, composition, rubrics and self-regulation. The audit's top 15 are all addressed, and the project rules hold (no movable do, no locks, a human-only rubato, no app-improvised soloist, hands-free, one screen). The three problems below are factual or rule contradictions that would lead a builder to build the wrong thing.

## (2) Substantive issues

**M1. §17.1: wrong death year for Razaf, so the legal example is wrong (major, legal)**
- **Problem:** the spec says "Honeysuckle Rose is not such a case, since Razaf died in 1953". Andy Razaf died in **1973**. STANDARDS_LEGAL line 122 agrees ("Razaf (1973)").
- **Why it matters:** Waller died in 1943 and Razaf in 1973, so Honeysuckle Rose is exactly the composer-died-by-1955, lyricist-died-later case. Someone setting tiers from this sentence could put it in 1A ("everyone died ≤ 1955") and serve it worldwide. In the EU and other life+70 countries it is protected until 2044. The error also affects the Scrapple from the Apple contrafact gate (§8.4), which depends only on the PD side.
- **Fix:** use Honeysuckle Rose and Ain't Misbehavin' as examples alongside Tea for Two. Add a guard test: a head's tier uses the latest death year among all credited authors, lyricists included, unless the lawyer rules otherwise.

**M2. §16 syllable rule vs lyrics: voice learn-a-tune has no text step (major, contradiction and pedagogy)**
- **Problem:** §16 says "every sung step (learn-a-tune … and the rest) uses a neutral syllable, degree numbers or letters". §8.4 step 2 and the voice variant follow that rule. But §8.1 and §11.2 serve verified ≤ 1930 lyrics "used in voice items", and §11.4 sets a phrase to words.
- **Why it matters:** as written, a guard test would forbid lyrics. A singer would then learn My Blue Heaven to the memory step without ever singing the words. For singers, the lyric is the core of a jazz tune (phrasing, breaths, back-phrasing in §11.2 all depend on it).
- **Fix:** limit the syllable rule to pitch-training steps; its purpose is to keep movable do out. In the voice variant, add "sing with the lyric (where a servable lyric exists)" after the sung melody step and before memory, and make memory include the text. Scat syllables are also exempt.

**M3. §8.4 / §8.7 / §13.6: the lick-bank soloist "as they are" has no rule for fitting the changes (major, musical validity)**
- **Problem:** the trading-4s partner and the responsive-comping soloist play bank licks "as they are", but nothing says the licks must match the bars' harmony.
- **Why it matters:** a builder can satisfy the rule with a random lick in the wrong key or over the wrong progression. The learner would then trade with, or comp behind, lines that clash with the form. That teaches the wrong ear.
- **Fix:** pick only a lick whose source progression matches the 4 bars' chords (functional match), and transpose it to the key. Transposing and choosing are not "generative phrasing". If nothing matches, fall back to drums only or silence, as already specified.

## (3) Minor notes
- **Harry Brooks' death year:** §8.2 says Brooks died in 1981; STANDARDS_LEGAL says 1970. The tier (1B) doesn't change, but reconcile it before tiers are recorded.
- **Choro 1A source (§8.2, §17.3):** Pixinguinha died in 1973, so no print of his can be 1A ("life term permitting" never holds until 2043). Point to Ernesto Nazareth (died 1934; Odeon is already in STANDARDS_LEGAL) and Chiquinha Gonzaga (died 1935).
- **"Tunes you know" (§13.5), "graded by pitch":** say how. Either give a tonic or key reference first, or grade the intervals so any key is accepted. Absolute grading would fail a learner who sings correctly in another key.
- **Head melody timing (§8.2 / §8.4):** only dotted and swung pairs are accepted. At band 3+, add a loose-timing tolerance (anticipations, laid-back entries), or label the melody step "as written" and leave interpretation to rubrics and the story ladder. Otherwise idiomatic head playing gets marked wrong.
- **King Porter Stomp as a "core head":** it is a multi-strain piano stomp, not a song-form head. Use its trio strain for non-piano learn-a-tune, or let the difficulty card place it.
- **Jazz brushes:** there is no brush content for drums (a core ballad skill). Add at least a brief plus a self or teacher-rated item ("needs MIDI" / acoustic).
- **SP §16.2 vs SP A1:** swing (F1) starts from about ♩ 90, while guide tones run "swung ♩=80". This is harmless for long notes, but say that below 90 a native-swing item keeps swing grading only where it has 8ths.
- **§14 PD repertoire:** "published ≤ 1930" alone isn't enough outside the US. State that pieces go through `servable()` and its tiers like heads do. Every composer currently listed is fine.
- **§8.4 / §1.10:** say plainly that the non-transfer steps on a *first-time* head also go to coverage. The text only covers "a head the learner has already played", which leaves the first pass ambiguous.
