## 3. Beyond the checklist

1. **The lick library is small.** licks.ts has 27 licks in 8 contexts. Contexts with no licks at all: turnaround, rhythm-changes bridge, Coltrane/pentatonic, minor-6 (Barry minor) and 3–♭9 in major. The library is the only rule-free vocabulary source on guitar, bass and voice, so it carries more weight than on piano.
2. **Piano/guitar beginner jazz on-ramp.** No early (~900–1050) level has a swing-8ths scale or arpeggio with the click on 2&4, or a "comp a basic blues in shells" in 12 keys. voicings-6 is 5 keys, and the jazz families start around shells ii–V–I.
3. **Bass gaps compared with piano/guitar Harris work:** brothers, triad pairs, digital patterns and Coltrane lines are absent from b-*. b-barry has no brothers level (0 hits for "brother" in bass*.ts).
4. **Guitar has no two-feel comping** (0 hits for two-feel in guitar*.ts), although the bass and drums courses teach 2 → 4 on the bridge. A guitarist in a trio needs it.
5. **No "outside" / side-slipping on piano or bass.** It exists only as guitar fusion (g-prog-7). Advanced jazz (1600+) usually covers side-slipping a ii–V a half step up and back.
6. **The improv rules have no Harris or enclosure-type rules beyond "enclosures".** Missing rule cards: "scale down, chord tones on beats (Harris)", "approach from above", "sequence the motif". They would make the improv studio check the same skills the drills teach (Everything connects).
7. **The ear course has no "hear the device" counterpart to several performed drills.** Examples: triplet turns, the 3–♭9 cell, enclosure type, and laid back vs on top inside a band context (swing-ratio 4 exists, but solo instrument only). Each new performed family above should get a matching ear level.
