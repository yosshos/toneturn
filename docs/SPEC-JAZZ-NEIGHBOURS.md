# Jazz and its neighbours: Brazilian, Afro-Cuban, soul-jazz, New Orleans, a Mediterranean touch (draft for the owner, 2026-11-22)

**What the owner said (2026-11-22):**
- The app is jazz first.
- Mediterranean rhythms and patterns stay only as a touch.
- Since the app is becoming more universal, add jazz-adjacent styles, and Latin jazz of every kind. These exist in the app but need to be expanded.
- **Priority (approved):** Brazilian and Afro-Cuban first (the biggest gap on piano and guitar), then soul-jazz and funk, New Orleans, and the Mediterranean touch last.
- **Order of work:** after the Practice build phases (BUILD-PLAN-2026-11), and before the languages, so each language arrives with the styles already in.

## 0. What exists (inventory, 2026-11-22)

| Where | Exists |

| Drums | d-latin (7: bossa, samba, son and rumba clave, cascara, cha-cha bell, 6/8 bembé), d-funk (8), d-linear (5), d-mideast (9: malfuf, maqsum, baladi, sa'idi, chiftetelli…) |

## 1. The principle

- **Jazz is the core.** Each neighbour style connects to jazz repertoire and to the way jazz uses it:
  - bossa standards;
  - Latin jazz tunes;
  - soul-jazz;
  - stride as the root of jazz piano;
  - odd meters as in Brubeck.
- **Every style is a package that goes through the four places** (like everything else, "everything connects"):

| Place | What the style brings |
|---|---|
| Technique | grooves and comping patterns per instrument, in all 12 keys, in the per-key matrix (a new block) |
| Licks | the style's vocabulary (montuno and guajeo figures, bossa lines, soul-jazz licks) |
| Repertoire | pieces tagged by style (public domain, or chords only, under US law), a style tab |
| Improvise | the band in the style; rules like "play over the clave" or "phrases that start on the 2 of the clave"; improvisation levels in the style |
| Ear | a style-recognition game (bossa / samba / cha-cha / mambo / songo / 6/8); hearing the clave (3-2 or 2-3) |
| Path | units at the top of each course (gated by background, like jazz), opened when climbing |

- **Legal:** rhythms and comping patterns are not protected. We write them ourselves from the known conventions. Pieces must be public domain or chords only.
- **Only verified theory:** every pattern is checked against a source (the research before each style, as in Harris). Anything unverified stays out.

## 2. Brazilian (first)

| Instrument | Content |
|---|---|
| Piano | bossa comping (bass on 1 and 3, chords syncopated in the bossa rhythm), voicings in fourths and 9ths; samba comping; partido-alto |
| Guitar | the bossa batida (thumb on the bass, the fingers syncopated); samba; samba-jazz; the bossa chords on the neck |
| Bass | bossa (1–5 with the dotted rhythm); samba (the surdo pattern); baião |
| Drums | bossa (cross-stick), samba, samba-jazz, baião (expanding d-latin) |
| The band | bossa (exists, refined), samba, samba-jazz, baião |
| Repertoire | bossa standards as chords only; public-domain choro for melody (to check per tune) |

## 3. Afro-Cuban and Latin jazz (first)

- **The base is clave literacy:**
  - son and rumba clave, 3-2 and 2-3;
  - hearing which way the clave runs;
  - playing every pattern "on the clave".

| Instrument | Content |
|---|---|
| Piano | **montuno / guajeo** (2- and 4-bar patterns on ii–V and I–IV–V, in the clave direction), cha-cha, mambo, bolero, songo comping, 6/8 afro-cubano |
| Guitar | guajeo and tumbao on guitar, the bolero accompaniment |
| Bass | tumbao (exists, expanding), cha-cha, mambo, bolero, 6/8 |
| Drums | cáscara, the cha-cha bell, mambo, songo, 6/8 bembé (exists, expanding), mozambique |
| The band | cha-cha, mambo, bolero, songo, 6/8 afro-cubano, Latin jazz (instead of the one generic "latin") |
| Improvise | the clave rules; trading over a montuno |

## 4. Soul-jazz, gospel, funk-jazz

- **Piano:** gospel chords (IV/I, moving inner voices), shuffle and boogaloo comping, Hammond-organ soul-jazz (k-organ exists).
- **Guitar and bass:** soul-jazz shuffle, boogaloo, funk.
- **The band:** soul-jazz, boogaloo, gospel 6/8, funk-jazz.

## 5. New Orleans

- **Piano:** stride (exists in comping, expanding), boogie-woogie.
- **Drums:** second line.
- **Bass:** the 2-feel and tuba-style bass.
- **The band:** second line, trad.

## 6. The Mediterranean touch (last, as a touch)

- **What stays:** the existing drum levels (d-mideast).
- **Added:** odd meters as jazz uses them: 7/8, and 9/8 as 2+2+2+3 (as in Blue Rondo à la Turk).
- **Improvise:** a phrygian dominant / hijaz colour as a rotation option on V7 → minor (already in the menus).
- **Not added:** a separate unit.

## 7. Order of work (after BUILD-PLAN-2026-11, before the languages)

1. Research per style: the patterns from sources, with every claim tagged [V] / [C] / [?].
2. **The band:** a real style for each (in place of the generic "latin"): bossa, samba, cha-cha, mambo, bolero, songo, 6/8.
3. **Piano and guitar:** the new Brazilian and Afro-Cuban families, in 12 keys, into the per-key matrix as a style block.
4. **The clave everywhere:** the rhythm training, the improvisation rules, the style-recognition game.
5. Repertoire by style, and the licks of each style.
6. Then soul-jazz and funk, New Orleans, and the Mediterranean touch.
7. **For every style:** course openers and unit openers, the skill map, the level test (the course change checklist), the guard tests.
