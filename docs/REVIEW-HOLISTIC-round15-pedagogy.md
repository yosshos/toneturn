## Substantive issues

### 1. §22 recording test: the "one rule" drops two US terms that the project's own research names (major, legal)
- **Section:** HOLISTIC §22 (replaces §8.1 tests (b)/(c) and the §8.10 phrase source flags); it also feeds the §10.1 listening canon and §10.2/§8.10 transcription flags.
- **Problem:** §22 treats any unpublished work on a ≤1925 side (improvisation, arrangement) as servable when its author died in 1955 or earlier. Under 17 U.S.C. §303(a), and as docs/research/PD-JAZZ-SOURCES.md §3.3 states:
  - (a) a pre-1978 unpublished work that was **published with authorization between 1978 and 2002** is protected **through 2047**, whatever the author's death year;
  - (b) an unpublished **work made for hire** (for example a dance-band arranger on staff, a salaried studio player) runs **120 years from creation**, not life+70. A 1925 creation is protected to the end of 2045.
- **Why it matters:** the guard test ("arranger died 1964 → never servable") would pass while the rule still serves protected material.
  - **Concrete risk:** the first canon candidate in §10.1, Jelly Roll Morton's 1923–24 solo piano, has a well-known 1982 published edition transcribed from his recordings (Dapogny, *The Collected Piano Music*, Smithsonian / G. Schirmer). If that edition was authorized, the transcribed improvised passages fall under the 2047 rule. Morton's 1941 death year does not clear them.
  - Arrangements on 1920s band sides are exactly where a work-for-hire claim is likely.
- **Fix:** add two conditions to the §22 test for every unpublished work:
  - "no authorized publication 1978–2002 (else protected through 2047)";
  - "not a work made for hire (else 120 years from creation)".
  - Where either is unknown, the recording is not servable. This keeps it conservative, as the rest of §8.1 is.
  - Add two guard tests:
    - a side whose improvisation was published in 1982 is never servable;
    - a side carrying a work-for-hire arrangement from 1925 is never servable before 2046.
  - Put the Morton edition check on the §17.3 canon checklist.

- **Contrafact pairings:** correct, including the Scrapple note (A sections from Honeysuckle Rose, bridge from I Got Rhythm).
- **Learn-a-tune order** is standard and well sequenced: listen → sing → melody → roots → guide tones → shells → cold memory → keys → solo. The band-1 and instrument variants (bass step 6, guitar chord-melody at band 3, voice sung roots and guide tones) are idiomatic.
- **Grading criteria** are musically valid and measurable as specified:
  - accompaniment graded against the beat map, register overlap and phrase-end density;
  - release-on-cue for fermatas;
  - "tunes you know" by starting degree plus intervals, octave free;
  - swing bands and the loose head timing at band 3+;
  - buzz rolls graded on quality.
- **Project rules are respected:**
  - no movable do (fixed do allowed only as note letters);
  - no app-improvised soloist (bank licks are matched to the chords and played as-is);
  - human-only rubato and expression models;
  - no locks (capstones and tune gates can be skipped, and a missing prerequisite skips a step);
  - hands-free (the exceptions are named);
  - phone screen limits (4–8-bar windows).
