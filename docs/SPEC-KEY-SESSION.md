# The key session: a full plan per key (draft for the owner, 2026-11-22)

Owner, 2026-11-22: practice by key must be a real workout. That means:
- the scale, then 3rds, triads and 4-note chords along the whole scale, and beyond;
- a short chromatic lick before each one: one chromatic degree below, then down from one chromatic degree above;
- Hanon, hand independence, contrary motion, two hands at different dynamics;
- everything at a rising tempo. When the coach sees the player's limit on one kind of exercise, it moves on.

## 1. The ladder of one key (each rung along the whole scale, 2 octaves unless the tier says otherwise)

### A. The scale
- **A1. Linear:** each hand (T0–T1), hands together (T2+), 4 octaves in 16ths (T5+).
- **A2. Contrary motion** (T2+). Hands a 3rd, 6th and 10th apart (T4+) [V ABRSM].
- **A3. Two hands at different dynamics** (T3+): RH forte with LH piano, then the other way round. Uses the existing control/balance kind.
- **A4. Rhythms and accents** (T3+): dotted, groups of 3 and 4, a moving accent (scales-11).

### B. The scale harmonised: every step goes through every degree
- **B1. Diatonic 3rds:** broken, then together [V]. In C: C-E, D-F, E-G …
- **B2. Triads on every degree:** arpeggiated, then block, then inversions (T4+). In C: CEG, DFA, EGB …
- **B3. 7th chords on every degree:** arpeggiated, then block with the LH root, then drop 2 (T5+). In C: CEGB, DFAC …
- **B4. 1-2-3-5 cells through the scale** (T3+) [V Premier Guitar]: C-D-E-G, D-E-F-A, E-F-G-B …
- **B5. 9th arpeggios through the scale** (T5+) [C]: C-E-G-B-D, D-F-A-C-E …

### C. The approach licks: the owner's method (2026-11-22). This replaces the research draft.

A lick comes before each shape on its way through the scale: before each 3rd, triad or 7th chord.
- Two rules hold for every lick:
  - **From below** is always a chromatic half step below the target note.
  - **From above** is always the next scale degree (diatonic). The one exception is the chromatic note in lick 3.
- **Going up:** the lick targets the shape's LOWEST note, then the shape is played up.
- **Going down:** the lick targets the shape's TOP note, then the shape is played down.

**Going up (triads in C major; target = the root):**
- **Lick 1, half step below:** B – C E G. Then the next degree: C♯ – D F A (Dm), and so on up the scale.
- **Lick 2, the enclosure:** below then above, B D – C E G, or above then below, D B – C E G. For Dm: C♯ E – D F A, or E C♯ – D F A.
- **Lick 3, the long enclosure:** below, scale-step above, chromatic above, below, then the target:
  - C: B D D♭ B – C E G;
  - Dm: C♯ E E♭ C♯ – D F A.

**Going down (from the C triad down the scale; target = the top note):**
- **Lick 1:** G♭ – G E C; then the B diminished triad: E – F D B. (The owner said "Bm7♭5": the 7th-chord ladder's chord on B. In the triad ladder it is B°.)
- **Lick 2:** F♯ A – G E C, or A F♯ – G E C. On B°: E G – F D B, or G E – F D B.
- **Lick 3:** F♯ A A♭ F♯ – G E C; on B°: E G G♭ E – F D B.

**The same licks run on the 3rds and the 7th chords.** Going up they target the lower note or the root; going down the upper note or the 7th.

**The order of difficulty:**
1. lick 1;
2. lick 2 (both orders);
3. lick 3.

Each one is played up the scale, then down. The shapes are taken in the ladder's order: 3rds, triads, 7th chords.

## 1b. More exercises in the key (the owner asked for a thorough list)

The standard technique catalogue, mapped to what exists. "+" marks something to add.

- **Hands apart at an interval:**
  - a 3rd, 6th or 10th apart (exists);
  - a 10th apart in both directions, with contrary motion back (exists);
  - hands an octave apart (the default).
- **Octaves:**
  - scale octaves in one hand, RH and LH (exists, advanced-1…3);
  - + broken octaves through the scale.
- **Double notes in one hand:** 3rds and 6ths (exists, advanced-4…8); + 6ths in the key at lower tiers.
- **Hand independence on the scale:**
  - one hand legato with the other staccato (exists as handArt);
  - one hand forte with the other piano (balance, exists);
  - + a canon, the second hand entering a beat or a bar later;
  - + scale against a LH ostinato in the key (k-indep is in C only today: put it in the key).
- **Finger independence:**
  - Hanon in the key (exists, fingers-15…18);
  - held notes (k-indep, ind-hold);
  - trills on each scale degree (exists, fingers);
  - repeated notes (exists).
- **Leaps:**
  - + scale degrees in leaps (an octave or two between steps);
  - LH bass leaps in the key's cadence I–IV–V–I as waltz or stride bass (exists in advanced-5/8, in C only today: put it in the key);
  - + chord leaps (block I–IV–V–I in two registers).
- **Broken chords in the key:**
  - Alberti I–IV–V7–I (exists, accomp-1);
  - broken I–IV–V in each inversion (exists, arpeggios broken/blocked).
- **Arpeggios in the key:** I in each inversion, V7 and vii°7 (exist); 4 octaves at T5+.
- **Dynamics:**
  - crescendo out and diminuendo back, accents, p, f, staccato (the dynamics lens on any rung);
  - terraced dynamics (exists in control).
- **Cadences in leaps and inversions:** I–IV–I6/4–V7–I in the three top positions (cadences-2), then the ii–V–I voicings (k-cadlib).

## 1c. Priority: where the player needs it (owner 2026-11-22)

- **The key comes from the player:** their choice, or the key of the week.
- **Inside the key, the coach picks the rungs to work on in this order:**
  1. a rung never touched at the player's tier;
  2. a rung played at a lower share of its target tempo;
  3. a rung last played longest ago.
  - The rung that was just passed is not picked again.
- **Over time the key cards show which keys are weaker** (they already colour by status). The key of the week prefers the weakest. The key page also marks the rung to strengthen next.

## 2. How the coach climbs and moves on

- **Every rung starts** at the tier's tempo, or just under the player's best for that rung in this key.
- **The climb:** two clean takes raise the tempo about 6%; an easy clean take raises it about 12%; "almost" holds the tempo.
- **The limit**, decided per rung and per key: the coach moves to the next rung when either
  - the rung's target tempo is played clean; or
  - the tempo has not gone up in the last 3 takes at the top (a plateau: two takes that miss at a new step, back to the last clean tempo, and a third that does not climb).
  - In both cases the rung's best clean tempo and its ceiling are saved for the key. Next time the rung starts from there, and the key page shows them.
- **A rung passed at its target in this key** opens its harder form (e.g. triads arpeggiated → block → inversions).

## 3. What one session holds (about 15 minutes), and the week

- **Every session:**
  - A1 at the tier, as the warm-up;
  - the next open rung of B;
  - one block in rotation from C, D or E (and F where it is open);
  - a closing visit back to the B rung at a higher tempo (interleaving [V Abushanab & Bishara 2013, see COACH-ADAPTIVE-RESEARCH]).
- **About 5–6 rungs a session, each visited until its limit or target.**
- **The week:** the key of the week cycles through all the blocks. By the end of the week every rung open at the player's tier has been visited at least twice.
- **The dynamics lens** applies to any scale, arpeggio or pattern rung.
